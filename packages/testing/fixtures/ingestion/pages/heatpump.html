Heat pumps outsell gas boilers for the first time

For the first time, installers sold more heat pumps than gas boilers during a full quarter, according to figures published by the national heating association on Tuesday. The shift was driven by new subsidies for older houses and by the rising price of gas over the last two winters.

Installers said the biggest change was in apartment buildings, where owners' associations now replace a central boiler with a group of heat pumps on the roof. Such projects used to fail on noise complaints, but the newest outdoor units are quiet enough to be installed next to bedroom windows.

The association warned that the country still lacks trained technicians. Training courses are full until next summer, and customers in rural regions often wait several months for an installation date. The government has promised to fund additional courses at vocational schools.

Critics pointed out that heat pumps only reduce emissions if the electricity grid keeps getting cleaner, and asked for faster approval of new wind farms.

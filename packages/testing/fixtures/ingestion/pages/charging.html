Motorway charging network doubles its capacity

The operator of the national motorway charging network has doubled the number of fast chargers along the main east–west corridor. Every service area now offers at least eight charging points rated at 150 kilowatts or more, and the busiest sites near the capital have twenty.

Drivers had complained for months about queues during holiday weekends, when a single broken charger could leave a dozen cars waiting. The new sites are built with spare grid capacity and battery buffers, so that a local power cut or a peak in demand no longer switches every charger to half power at once.

The operator also changed its pricing. Instead of charging per minute, which penalised older cars that charge slowly, customers now pay per kilowatt-hour, with a small idle fee that starts ten minutes after charging has finished. Payment by contactless card is available at every charger, without an app or a membership.

Consumer groups said the changes were overdue and asked the government to require the same standards on regional roads, where many villages still have no public charger at all.

Why the tail of Comet Arden turned green

By Leila Farouk · September 22, 2026

Astronomers have explained why the tail of Comet Arden, which has been visible to the naked eye in the northern hemisphere for the past two weeks, suddenly turned a vivid green last Thursday. The colour comes from diatomic carbon, a fragile molecule released as the comet's icy nucleus warms on its approach to the Sun.

Diatomic carbon glows green when sunlight excites it, but it is usually destroyed within about two days, long before it can drift far from the head of the comet. That is why most comets show a green head and a white or bluish tail. According to a team at the Hallberg Observatory, a series of small outbursts on the comet's surface released so much gas at once that the molecule reached the tail before it broke apart.

"We were lucky to catch it," said Dr. Samuel Oyelaran, who leads the observatory's comet programme. "The green tail lasted less than a day. By Friday evening it had already faded to the usual pale colour." His team captured more than three hundred images during the event and has shared them with other observatories.

The comet will make its closest approach to the Sun on October 3 and should remain visible with binoculars until the middle of the month. Astronomers recommend looking low in the western sky shortly after sunset, away from city lights.

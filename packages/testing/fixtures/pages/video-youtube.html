Volunteers finish restoring the 1912 tram

By Ivo Marek · September 24, 2026

After four years of weekend work, a group of volunteers has finished restoring the oldest surviving tram of the Riverton network. Car number 7 left the factory in 1912 and carried passengers until 1958, when it was turned into a garden shed on a farm outside the city.

The team rebuilt the wooden body almost entirely from new oak, keeping the original brass fittings wherever they could be saved. The electrical equipment came from a sister car that was scrapped in Vienna, and the seats were copied from photographs in the city archive.

"The hardest part was the bogies," said Lena Horváthová, who led the restoration. "When we lifted the body we found that the axle boxes had rusted solid, and we had to machine new ones ourselves." The city lent the group a workshop in the old depot and paid for the new wheels.

The tram will run on the heritage line along the river on the first Sunday of every month from October. Tickets cost two euros, and the money will go towards the restoration of a second car, a 1928 trailer that is still standing in a museum yard.
